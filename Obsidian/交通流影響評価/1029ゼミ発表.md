# 背景
- 自動運転技術はいろいろなことが期待されている
- そのため世界中で開発が進んでいるが，わが国でも全国各地で実証実験が行われている
- しかしあまり実用化に至っていないのが現状
	- 技術的な課題やリスクに対する懸念がある
	- 安全性を担保したうえで実用化していくかが重要
- また，3つの理由から本研究では「"低速の"自動運転バス」に着目した．
	- 1・2つめはバスの理由．3つめは低速の理由
- 一つ目の理由として，バスの運転手不足の問題がある．
	- バスの運転手がこのくらい減っていて，都市部でさえ路線の減便も起こっている
	- 自動運転技術はこの問題を解決することが期待されている．
- 2つ目の理由として，バスは路線が決まっているので，ルートの決まっていない自家用車と比べてインフラと連携した自動運転がやりやすい．
- 3つ目の理由として，バスは車内事故防止や快適性の観点からより慎重な走行が求められている
	- シートベルトを着けない
	- 立ち客もいる
- しかし，安全性を重視しすぎると円滑性が低下すると考えられる
- そこで本研究では，速度が小さく，加速度の小さい穏やかな運転挙動をする自動運転バスと，他の手動運転車が混在する際に，交通流の円滑性がどのようになるか分析する
	- これにより，今後の実証実験や実用化に向けて講じるべき施策の立案に資することが期待される．

# 本研究のまとめ
## 本研究の貢献
・柏の葉地域で観測した交通量や旅行速度，駐停車車両数などのデータを用いて，現況再現モデルを作成し，現況再現性を確認した．

・構築したシミュレータを使用し，バスをAVバスに置き換え，一部のリンクでは3段階の速度設定のシナリオ下で交通流シミュレーションを行った．

・低速AVバスが混入することにより，後続車の速度低下や交差点遅れ，迂回行動などによりネットワーク全体，あるいは局所的に円滑性が低下することが示された．

・時間帯ごとに評価指標を分析することにより，交通状態によっては低速AVバスの影響が異なることを示した．
## 本研究の課題
今後の課題としては〇〇挙げられる．
- 対象リンクのみで自動走行させること
	- 本研究ではシミュレータの都合上，対象リンク以外でも，すべてのバスは表●の自動運転バスの運転挙動パラメータで走行し，対象リンクではさらに速度についても制限が入るという風になっていた
	- 実際にはレベル4自動運転とは，「限定空間で人がいない走行をすること」であり，この状態を再現した分析をすることが実証実験や実用化につながると考えられる
- 次に，より再現性の高いシミュレーションを行うことである．
	- 旅行速度についての現況再現性を向上させること．
	- 特に，AVのパラメータ設定については各研究によって異なっていて，「そのパラメータが自動運転車であることを保証する」ようなパラメータ設定については未解決の問題である．柏の葉エリアでは，レベル4の実証走行が行われる予定であるため，そこで得られるAVsの走行履歴からパラメータを推定するといったような，実証実験との連携が一つの方法として考えられる． 
　- 駐停車車両についても、全域ではなく局所的に施策を導入したうえで効果を評価する必要がある。また，さらに交通量の多いエリアの場合駐停車車両とAVバスの組み合わせによる影響が出やすくなる可能性もあるため，エリアを変えての分析も今後の課題である．  
# 今後の展望
- また、低速AVバスは交通流のペースメーカーとして機能するため，信号との協調により、交通流の速度は抑えつつも交差点遅れを減らすことで流量は維持するという，交通流の新たな制御方法は，十分に検討されるべき課題である．


# Background

- Various expectations are placed on autonomous driving technology.
    
- For that reason, development is progressing all over the world, and demonstration experiments are being conducted throughout Japan as well.
    
- However, in reality, full-scale practical implementation has not yet been achieved.
    
    - There are still technical issues and concerns about risks.
        
    - The key challenge is how to achieve practical implementation while ensuring safety.
        
- In this study, we focus on **“low-speed autonomous buses”** for three main reasons: two relating to buses themselves, and one relating to low speed.
    
- The first reason is the shortage of bus drivers.
    
    - The number of bus drivers has been decreasing significantly, and even in urban areas, service reductions are occurring.
        
    - Autonomous driving technology is expected to help solve this issue.
        
- The second reason is that, because bus routes are fixed, infrastructure-linked autonomous driving is easier to implement than for private cars, whose routes are not fixed.
    
- The third reason is that buses must drive more cautiously for reasons of in-vehicle safety and passenger comfort:
    
    - Passengers do not wear seatbelts.
        
    - There are also standing passengers.
        
- However, if too much emphasis is placed on safety, traffic smoothness may decrease.
    
- Therefore, this study analyzes how overall traffic smoothness changes when low-speed, low-acceleration autonomous buses and manually driven vehicles coexist.
    
    - The results are expected to contribute to planning countermeasures for future demonstration experiments and practical implementation.

# Methodology

## Target Network

- The study area is **Kashiwa-no-ha**, chosen because:
    
    - The focus is on **urban-type congestion** caused by intersection delays, parking avoidance, and detours — not on highways or rural regions.
        
    - Autonomous shuttle buses are already in operation, and level-4 implementation is being considered. Thus, the results may contribute directly to ongoing demonstrations and practical applications.
        
- The actual network is shown here.
    
    - Only the target links are assigned speed restrictions for autonomous buses.
        
- The OD demand data are as follows, including:
    
    - Bus OD pairs and vehicle counts, total traffic volume including private vehicles, and other basic conditions.
        

## Model Description

- The simulation uses the **MicroAVENUE** microscopic traffic simulator.
    
    - The car-following model is **IDM+**, which determines acceleration based on distance and relative speed to the leading vehicle.
        
    - The governing equation is shown here.
        
- Additional models include:
    
    - Route choice model
        
    - Lane change model
        
    - On-street parking model
        
    - Parking avoidance model
        
    - (Detailed descriptions are provided in the appendix.)
        

## Calibration of the Current Situation

- Free-flow speed, capacity, and jam density were assigned according to road characteristics.
    
- Traffic volume surveys were conducted at signalized intersections in October 2023.  
    OD demands were estimated from the aggregated results, and signal parameters were set based on the observed control patterns.
    
- The simulated results based on these OD and signal data are shown here — they reproduced field conditions reasonably well.
    
- For travel speed validation, ETC2.0 probe data were used to calculate observed average travel times, which were compared against simulation outputs. Agreement was fair, though accuracy varied by link.
    

## Evaluation Scenarios

- Eight scenarios (**Table 2**) were analyzed.
    
- Scenarios 1 and 2 consist entirely of manually driven vehicles (MVs).
    
- Scenarios 3–8 replace all buses with autonomous vehicles (AVs).
    
- In Scenarios 5 and 6, the desired speeds of AVs on target links are set to **70%** of the normal value (low-speed), and in Scenarios 7 and 8, to **50%** (very low-speed) — as shown in **Figure 1**.
    
- Odd-numbered scenarios include on-street parked vehicles, whereas even-numbered scenarios assume full enforcement of no-parking policies or bus-bay improvements.
    
    - This setting reflects the fact that AV buses and their following or adjacent vehicles can experience large speed differences when avoiding parked vehicles, leading to queuing delays. The even scenarios are introduced to evaluate this effect.
        
- Simulations were run from **6:30 to 18:30 on October 18, 2023**, matching survey conditions.  
    For each scenario, 20 runs were made with randomized OD generation and route choice parameters (18 valid runs after excluding buggy cases).
    

## Evaluation Indicators and Scales

- Indicators:
    
    - **Space mean speed**, **total travel time (TTT)**, and **total travel distance**.
        
    - Speed and TTT represent smoothness, while total distance also reflects **the extent of detour behavior**.
        
- Evaluation scales:
    
    - Entire network over all hours
        
    - Every 15 minutes across all links
        
    - Each link over the entire period
        
    - Specific time–space zones

# Results

## Network-wide Analysis

- (Overall)
    
    - With the introduction of AV buses and low-speed AVs, average speed decreased and TTT increased.
        
    - Total travel distance remained nearly unchanged.
        
    - Differences due to the presence or absence of parked vehicles were small at the whole-network level.
        
- (Hourly trend)
    
    - Differences in total travel distance among scenarios were minimal.
        
    - For speed:
        
        - Three observations:
            
            1. During peaks (8:00, 18:00), inter-scenario differences are large because the effects of low-speed AVs propagate to surrounding vehicles.
                
            2. Even during the 17:00 peak, differences are small, suggesting congestion already exists, limiting AV impact.
                
            3. During off-peak hours, differences are minimal because interactions are rare.
                
        - The number of buses is small compared to private cars, so fleet size likely had limited effect.
            
    - TTT showed similar patterns.

## Relationship between Parking and Detours

- Comparison between **no05–no06** and **no07–no08** under the same scale (common legend).
    
- **no06** shows stronger detour tendencies:
    
    - When parked vehicles exist, some drivers continue through the main route;  
        when parking is removed, some begin to detour.
        
    - This implies a **critical speed** below which such drivers cease to exist.
        
        - (Effect of parking on detour route) > (Effect of parking on target) + (Effect of low speed)
            
    - Conversely, when speeds are extremely low, even if parked vehicles exist on the detour, most drivers still choose that detour.  
        As a result, detour links become congested, **reducing total network capacity**.
        
        - (Effect of parking on detour) < (Effect of parking on target) + (Effect of low speed)

# Summary

## Contributions

- Constructed a validated simulation model of the **Kashiwa-no-ha** area using observed traffic volumes, travel speeds, and on-street parking data.
    
- Using this model, replaced buses with AV buses and tested multiple speed scenarios.
    
- Showed that introducing low-speed AV buses decreases smoothness both network-wide and locally due to following-vehicle slowdown, intersection delays, and detours.
    
- By analyzing time-of-day variations, demonstrated that AV bus impacts differ depending on traffic conditions.
    

## Limitations

- (1) Only the target links should be operated autonomously.
    
    - Due to simulator constraints, all buses used AV driving parameters, with speed restrictions only on target links.
        
    - In reality, level-4 automation is defined as operation in limited areas without human drivers.  
        Simulating such conditions would better align with demonstration experiments.
        
- (2) Need for higher-fidelity simulation.
    
    - Improve travel-speed calibration.
        
    - Parameter settings for AV driving behavior vary by study;  
        developing parameters that truly represent AVs remains unsolved.  
        Future collaboration with level-4 demonstration runs in Kashiwa could help estimate realistic parameters from trajectory data.
        
- (3) Evaluate localized parking-control measures rather than applying them network-wide.
    
    - In higher-demand areas, the interaction between parked vehicles and AV buses may be more pronounced.  
        Thus, future analyses in denser networks are needed.
        

## Future Outlook

- Low-speed AV buses can function as **traffic flow pacemakers**.  
    Coordinating them with traffic signals may suppress speeds while maintaining flow by reducing intersection delays.  
    Such **signal–AV coordination for flow control** should be explored further.
